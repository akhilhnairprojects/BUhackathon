06 · Do the patterns hold together? Risk model and validation¶

Purpose: (1) confirm that time of day, carrier, season and airport each matter after controlling for the others; (2) measure how predictable disruption is at booking time; (3) measure how much a short-notice weather signal adds. The model is a tool for explanation and for the risk look-up in the app, not an end in itself.

  • Booking-time model uses only what is known when the ticket is bought: carrier, origin, destination, month, day of week, departure hour, distance, holiday window, and the airport's typical adverse-weather frequency for that month (NOAA climatology).
  • Short-notice model adds the actual weather on the day at both ends (a stand-in for a 1-3 day forecast).
  • Leakage guard: no departure delay, no delay-cause fields, no tail numbers.
  • Validation: 5-fold cross-validation grouped by flight date, so a storm day is never in both training and test folds.

1. Fit and cross-validate four models¶

def logit(num):
    pre = ColumnTransformer([("cat", OneHotEncoder(handle_unknown="ignore", min_frequency=30), CAT), ("num", StandardScaler(), num)])
    return Pipeline([("pre", pre), ("clf", LogisticRegression(C=0.5, max_iter=2000))])

def hgb(num):
    pre = ColumnTransformer([("cat", OrdinalEncoder(handle_unknown="use_encoded_value", unknown_value=-1), CAT), ("num", "passthrough", num)])
    clf = HistGradientBoostingClassifier(max_iter=400, learning_rate=0.06, max_leaf_nodes=48, min_samples_leaf=100,
                                         categorical_features=list(range(len(CAT))), random_state=0)
    return Pipeline([("pre", pre), ("clf", clf)])

models = {"Logistic - booking time": (logit(NUM_BOOK), NUM_BOOK), "Boosted trees - booking time": (hgb(NUM_BOOK), NUM_BOOK),
          "Logistic - short notice (+weather)": (logit(NUM_SHORT), NUM_SHORT), "Boosted trees - short notice (+weather)": (hgb(NUM_SHORT), NUM_SHORT)}
res, preds = [], {}
for name, (m, num) in models.items():
    X = df[CAT + num]
    p = cross_val_predict(m, X, y, cv=cv, groups=groups, method="predict_proba")[:, 1]
    preds[name] = p
    res.append([name, roc_auc_score(y, p), average_precision_score(y, p), brier_score_loss(y, p)])
res = pd.DataFrame(res, columns=["model", "AUC", "avg_precision", "Brier"]).set_index("model")
res.loc["Always predict the base rate"] = [0.5, y.mean(), brier_score_loss(y, np.full_like(y, y.mean(), dtype=float))]
res.to_csv(prep.TABLES / "model_scores.csv")

Disruption is a noisy event - most of it is caused by things no one knows at booking time - so an AUC in the high 0.60s at booking time is what we should expect. The useful questions are whether the model is calibrated (a predicted 30% really means 30%) and how well it ranks choices, which is what a booking policy needs.

Takeaways¶

  • Every headline lever survives adjustment: departure hour, carrier, month and airport each shift the odds independently.
  • Booking-time predictions are well calibrated; the riskiest decile of bookings is disrupted several times as often as the safest decile - enough to act on.
  • Knowing the day's weather lifts predictive power materially: a forecast-triggered rebooking rule has real information behind it.
  • Limitation: one year of data; the model ranks risk but cannot predict individual delays.
